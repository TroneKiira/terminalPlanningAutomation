3.3.4.7. Level of Service Guidelines 
Exhibit 3.3.4.7 provides the LoS guidelines for each airport terminal facility/subsystem. The table provides 
guidelines to be used when undertaking capacity and LoS analyses. Guidelines have been supplemented to 
include facilities involving self-service processing and customs control. 
The LoS guidelines have been adjusted to better reflect the current global aviation market. It is recognized, 
however, that different regions, countries and markets require adjustments to the airport environment to better 
equate with their service needs. The revised LoS benchmarks now include a range of values for space and 
time to better enable an airport to tailor its service level to the market and region it serves.

the applicable LoS standards for those facilities.

Impact of COVID-19 on the Level of Service Guidelines 
See 1.8 COVID19 Green Pages for additional information 
From the very early stages of the pandemic, one of the key protective measures that have been implemented 
across almost every country is a requirement to maintain a physical distance that reduces the risk of 
transmission. Physical distancing is an effective measure to limit transmission of SARS-CoV-2 and should be 
part of a comprehensive package of measures to limit the spread of COVID-19. 
However, one of the key challenges of the pandemic has been the lack of consistent application of the health￾related measures globally. In particular, where requirements have been applied different recommendations in 
terms of physical distancing have been proposed ranging between 1 and 2 meters. 
In an attempt to propose a harmonized approach to physical distancing both the WHO and ICAO established 
a recommendation to keep a distance of at least 1m which, in combination with other health related 
regulations (use of face masks, vaccination certificates, optimizing passenger flows etc), should provide the 
means for a safe and healthy journey. 
While there are different interpretations on how to measure the physical distance and how to apply it to 
queuing and holding areas of terminal buildings, it appears to be clear that the objective is to avoid potential 
droplets/aerosols from the nose and mouth of one passenger teaching the passengers standing immediately 
next to them. Therefore, it is fair to make the interpretation that a direct linear separation between two 
Passengers should suffice in order to comply with a physical distancing requirement of 1 meter. 
With all of this in mind, the current Level of Service Guidelines are perfectly able to accommodate those 
minimum requirements without any changes. Only minor adjustments to the layouts of queueing or seating 
areas should be required to accommodate the minimum requirements set by the WHO and ICAO. 
It should also be noted that while we recognize that regulatory mandates vary across different countries and 
regions imposing more stringent separation requirements than the minimum, it is IATA's view that these 
measures are likely to be temporary and will be lifted once the vaccination rates are high and consistent 
globally. This is also consistent with what has been observed in those countries with high vaccination rates 
and low number of infections where physical distancing requirements have been lifted in other public 
buildings, modes of transport and interior spaces. 
In addition, the Level of Service guidelines also address queueing times and seating availability. Both of these 
areas have also been impacted by some of the health-related restrictions (see Impact of COVID-19 on 
Terminal Capacity). While it is recognized that during times with additional processes or extra processing 
times the queueing objective defined in the LOS framework may not be achievable, the temporary nature of 
those restrictions should not have an impact on queueing requirements defined in the Level of Service 
guidelines. Similarly, current seating design makes it impossible to maintain the separation distances, but it 
should also be seen as temporary measure. Advancements in self check in and security screening will 
increase queuing efficiency. Other technologies exist or are being developed to flatten peaks using virtual 
queuing.”

Asa conclusion, and taking all of the above into consideration, IATA does not see the need to change any of 
the recommended space, queueing time or seating requirements in the Level of Service guidelines. 
Note: Circulation and dwelling areas in passenger terminals have, in general, not been a capacity 
constraint. However, passengers are often less than 1m apart in these areas, in particular where 2-way flows 
and cross flows occur. Natural physical distancing of passengers may result in congestion occurring in such 
areas of conflict. Application of passenger guidance to create 1-way flows, such as using web barriers, may 
be appropriate to ease congestion as demand grows.
